## Tab 3: Call Prep

**Opener (Jordan, first 30 seconds):**
"Marcus, thanks for the time. I asked Dana to join because of something we keep seeing in your building. Our techs are in your ductwork every quarter, and the dampers up there sit between our world and the fire world. I wanted you to hear from Dana how Cardinal handles that at other hospitals."

**Discovery questions (Dana leads):**
1. Who tests your fire and smoke dampers today, and when was the last round?
2. How did your last Joint Commission or state survey go on the life safety side?
3. How many contractors touch life safety in this building, and who pulls their records together before a survey?
4. What would you change about your current fire contractor if the Q1 renewal were a clean slate?

**The one story to tell:**
A hospital of similar size had separate HVAC and fire contractors. Their damper testing fell through the gap for a full cycle, and it surfaced as a finding during a survey. After consolidating under Northfield, the same team that services the air handlers coordinates damper testing with Cardinal's alarm and sprinkler work, and the facilities director gets one binder before every survey.

**Ask to close the call:** a free walkthrough of their life safety records with Dana, before the Q1 renewal decision.

**Send after the call:**
- One-page case summary of the damper-gap story above
- Sample survey-ready records binder
- Northfield one-pager: "One team accountable for the whole building"
- Cardinal credentials: certified inspectors, response times, coverage map

## Tab 4: Objection Handling

**"We're happy with our current fire contractor."**
Good, then this isn't about replacing them for the sake of it. The question is whether anyone owns the dampers and the handoffs. A walkthrough of your records answers that either way.

**"Putting everything with one company feels risky."**
Fair. You'd still have specialist teams; Cardinal's techs are fire people, Keystone's are HVAC people. What changes is one point of accountability and one set of records. And you already know how Keystone performs.

**"We're locked in until the contract ends."**
That's why Q1 matters. Use the walkthrough now so you have a real comparison when the renewal decision comes up.

## Tab 5: Competitors & Battlecards

**Likely incumbent:** a national fire protection provider on a multi-year contract.

| | National incumbent | Northfield (Cardinal + Keystone) |
|---|---|---|
| Fire inspection and testing | Yes | Yes |
| Knows the building's HVAC | No | Keystone has serviced it since 2021 |
| Covers the damper gap | Rarely owns it | Owned end to end |
| Records for surveyors | Fire only | Fire, life safety, and HVAC in one set |
| Who shows up | Rotating techs | Local crews who know the site |
| Scheduling | Separate from HVAC | Coordinated with existing Keystone visits |

**How we win:** Don't compete on price or on fire capability alone. Compete on the gap. The incumbent can't offer coordination with an HVAC team it doesn't have.

**How we lose:** If this becomes a line-item price comparison on sprinkler inspections. Steer back to total risk and total coordination.

**Landmine to set:** "When your last damper testing happened, who coordinated access with the HVAC side?"
